# AI in Education Digest — 2026-07-13

**China — university communications office** — Staff at a Chinese university increasingly draft official communications in Chinese first, then run them through a mix of AI tools — ChatGPT, DeepSeek, and homegrown platforms Doubao and Kimi — before publishing in English, producing inconsistent spelling conventions, honorifics ("professor" versus "Dr") and tone across the institution's materials. The piece argues for a university-wide AI style guide paired with workshops and peer review to catch these inconsistencies before they reach students and international partners.
*For educators:* If different AI tools quietly encode different linguistic and cultural defaults, is your institution's "voice" still something educators design, or just whichever tool's default happened to be used that day?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/building-consistent-university-voice

**United Kingdom — Edinburgh Napier University** — A survey of more than 6,000 students at UK universities found most claim to be honest about their AI use, with 67% saying they would not use AI on an assessment if told not to — but 5.2% of habitual AI users admitted using it "most of the time" or "always" even when they knew it wasn't permitted.
*For educators:* If self-reported honesty and detected usage rates keep diverging across these studies, is asking students to self-disclose AI use a workable policy, or just a formality?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/majority-students-deny-using-ai-when-forbidden-study-finds

**United States — Merrimack College** — Education professor Dan Sarofian-Butin now spends the first ten minutes of every lecture having students log into a frontier AI model — ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini — and treats it as a standing "teaching assistant" for the rest of class, a practice he says took three years to refine into something that supports learning rather than replacing it.
*For educators:* If AI is introduced as a formal "teaching assistant" from minute one, does that framing change what students feel entitled to ask it — and what they stop bothering to ask you?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/i-start-my-lectures-introducing-my-teaching-assistant-genai
