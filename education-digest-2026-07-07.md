# Education Digest — July 7, 2026

**[United Kingdom]** — A year-long, Jisc-coordinated trial has 10 UK universities piloting "Graide," which drafts AI feedback and marks against a rubric and sample essays before an academic reviews and releases them, while 5 more institutions trial "TeacherMatic" for building teaching resources. Early findings complicate the sales pitch: keeping a human in the loop has proven "more complex than expected" and hasn't clearly saved academics time, with AI-based summative marking still judged "a long way off."
*For educators:* If AI-drafted feedback doesn't save you time once you factor in checking it, what would actually have to change — the tool, the workflow, or the expectation — for it to be worth adopting?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/ai-marking-trial-not-looking-replace-humans

**[United States — Georgia Tech]** — Georgia Tech's Center for 21st Century Universities argues AI shouldn't be bolted on as a generic course, but woven into the core practices of each discipline — for instance, accounting and finance moving from teaching manual reporting toward AI-enabled predictive auditing and fraud detection. Its College of Lifetime Learning is building "stackable" microcredential pathways so students and working professionals gain the "disciplinary fluency" to critique and vet AI output rather than just use it.
*For educators:* In your subject, what's the one core practice you'd redesign first if you assumed every student already had a capable AI assistant on day one?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/how-integrate-aienabled-lifelong-learning-across-disciplines

**[South Korea]** — Chonnam National University's "AI Campus Transformation" gives roughly 30,000 students, faculty and staff free access to eight generative AI tools — including ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Grok, Llama, Mistral, Perplexity and Qwen — that would otherwise cost each person around $140 a month combined. Korea University has taken a different tack, building a university-wide AI-integrated curriculum alongside a dedicated AI research institute, its own AI data center, and AI tutors.
*For educators:* Given a fixed budget, would your institution get more value from buying broad access to many AI tools, or from investing that money in fewer tools plus deeper curriculum integration?
