# AI in Education Digest — 2026-07-30

**China — Nanjing University, all undergraduate departments** — Times Higher Education reports that Nanjing University became the first Chinese institution to make artificial intelligence a compulsory element of every undergraduate's education, rolling out a "1+X+Y" system that pairs core AI lectures and seminars with department-specific electives and internships, applied across every faculty regardless of major.
*For educators:* If AI literacy became a graduation requirement at your institution tomorrow, would it work better as one shared core course for everyone, or as a discipline-specific module tailored separately by each department — and what would your subject's version look like?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/university-launches-compulsory-artificial-intelligence-module

**International — Companion's "Einstein AI" tool, higher education broadly** — Times Higher Education covered the launch and rapid takedown of "Einstein AI," a tool from startup Companion that offered to log into a student's virtual learning environment and complete coursework "while they slept" — watching lectures, posting in discussions and submitting assignments on their behalf; after backlash the company scrubbed the "do it while you sleep" language before pulling the product entirely following a trademark dispute over the Einstein name, not a regulatory intervention.
*For educators:* The tool disappeared because of a trademark complaint, not because anyone stopped the underlying capability — if a similarly capable but blandly-named agent launched tomorrow, what in your current assessment design would actually catch it?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/strange-case-einstein-ai-spotlights-chatbot-concerns

**United States — Gwinnett County Public Schools, Seckinger High School, Georgia** — EdSurge reports on a district-built "AI readiness" framework spanning six areas (data science, mathematical reasoning, creative problem solving, ethics, applied experience and programming), paired with a diagnostic assessment developed with the ISTE research team and piloted with roughly 1,200 students to identify exactly which skills students are missing before teachers plan instruction around them.
*For educators:* A generic "AI literacy" goal is hard to teach toward — if you had to pick just two of those six readiness areas to diagnose and target first in your own students, which two would reveal the biggest gaps?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/2025-06-09-a-district-a-diagnostic-and-a-drive-for-ai-readiness
