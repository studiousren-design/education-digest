# AI in Education Digest — 2026-10-20

**[Japan / Sophia University, English-medium classes]** — A Sophia University academic used action research to test a five-step "5P" framework for student AI use, and found that students applied the "prove" and "ponder" steps less consistently once deadlines piled up. The finding suggests that critical-use habits break down under time pressure rather than from lack of understanding.
*For educators:* If students skip verification when deadlines bite, which parts of your assignment calendar are quietly designing uncritical AI use?
Source: https://timeshighereducation.com/campus/help-undergraduates-sustain-critical-ai-use-under-deadline-pressure

**[Philippines & Hong Kong / De La Salle University, Lingnan University]** — De La Salle University has offered a bachelor's in philosophy and AI for two years, and its first-year intake for the coming year has reportedly quadrupled. Lingnan University in Hong Kong launched a similar degree last year, suggesting growing Asian demand for humanities-led AI education.
*For educators:* What would a philosophy-of-AI strand look like in your own discipline, rather than as a standalone degree?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/asia-embraces-philosophy-and-ai-courses

**[Australia / Student survey]** — A Studiosity survey of more than 1,200 Australian students found one in four worry they learn little or nothing from generative AI, yet 11 in 20 expect their university to supply AI tools. Students want the tools but doubt the learning.
*For educators:* How would you design a task where AI access is provided but the learning gain is demonstrably still the student's?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/students-worry-they-learn-nothing-ai-want-it-anyway
