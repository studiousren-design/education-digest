# Education Digest — September 23, 2026

**United States (K-12 edtech infrastructure)** — Learning Commons, a nonprofit backed by the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, launched an open platform giving edtech developers free access to machine-readable educational datasets, research-backed evaluations and instructional workflows, alongside six new partner organizations including Canva Education, MagicSchool and TalkingPoints. The goal is to let smaller developers build AI tools grounded in evidence about what actually works in classrooms, rather than each vendor re-deriving that evidence on its own.

*For educators:* If the underlying data and evaluations behind your school's AI tools all trace back to the same shared platform, does that make you more confident in them, or more worried about a single point of failure?

Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/learning-commons-launches-open-ai-platform-for-k-12-edtech

**United Kingdom (higher education)** — Universities UK's new "Future Jobs Roadmap" commits every undergraduate course to having an academic designated as an "AI trailblazer," and pledges that all undergraduates will have access to AI tools, alongside a broader promise that every student gets meaningful work-based learning by 2035. The plan, backed by Pearson and the British Chambers of Commerce, links AI fluency directly to graduate employability rather than treating it as a standalone literacy add-on.

*For educators:* If your department had to name one lecturer as its "AI trailblazer" tomorrow, would it be obvious who that should be — and what would everyone else be waiting on them for?

Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/all-undergraduates-should-get-work-based-learning-v-cs-say

**Asia-Pacific (Indonesia, Japan, Malaysia, Philippines, South Korea, Thailand and beyond)** — Times Higher Education's AI & Digital Maturity Index, drawing on over 1,500 responses from 433 universities across 19 Asia-Pacific systems, finds that most institutions now have AI strategies and infrastructure in place but struggle to move beyond that into everyday teaching and research practice. The index sorts institutions into four stages — Incidental, Intentional, Integrated and Optimised — with the region's real gap sitting between having a policy and actually changing what happens in classrooms.

*For educators:* Would you place your own classroom's AI use at "Intentional" or "Integrated" — and what's the one thing stopping it from moving up a level?
