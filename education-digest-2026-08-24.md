# Education Digest — 2026-08-24

**[United States]** — The U.S. Department of Education issued long-awaited federal guidance for states and districts on educational technology, insisting that "technology use is purposeful, instructionally sound, and contributing to measurable student outcomes" while leaving the specifics of AI rulemaking to states and local districts. That hands districts a federal framework to weigh against the AI policies many have already written on their own as the new school year begins.
*For educators:* If federal guidance sets only broad principles and leaves the details to states and districts, who should be accountable when a locally approved AI tool ends up failing students?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/department-of-education-issues-long-awaited-edtech-guidance-for-states-and-districts

**[United States — research funding]** — A study of more than 130,000 U.S. federal grant proposals to the NIH and NSF found that heavier use of large language models made proposals more likely to get funded — but also made them more generic, tending to resemble ideas that had already won funding before. Researchers warn that as AI-assisted writing becomes routine in graduate training and faculty research, it could be quietly narrowing the range of scientific ideas that make it through the funding pipeline.
*For educators:* If AI helps students and researchers write proposals that are more fundable but less original, how should research training and mentorship change to protect genuine intellectual risk-taking?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/large-language-models-reduce-originality-research-proposals

**[China]** — A new wave of "start-up" research universities in China — including Westlake University and the Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech) — are being built from scratch around English-medium instruction, early hands-on research, and curricula explicitly designed to prepare students to "ask meaningful questions, think independently, solve complex problems, and collaborate across disciplines" in the AI era. Eight such institutions now exist, with more in the pipeline, treating AI-ready pedagogy as a founding design principle rather than a retrofit.
*For educators:* If a university can be architected from day one around AI-era skills, what would your own institution need to cut or restructure to catch up rather than bolt AI on as an afterthought?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/chinas-start-universities-come-age
