# AI in Education Digest — September 11, 2026

**[United States — Indigenous Community School]** — An instructional technology coordinator at a tribal school describes treating AI as a tool for stewardship rather than extraction, using it to help digitize and organize community knowledge while drawing a hard line around who controls sacred material. The piece frames the work through the lens of quilting and ribbon-skirt making — technology stitched into a living culture "in a good way," not imposed on it.
*For educators:* If a community treats certain knowledge as sacred rather than public, what obligations does that place on any AI tool you bring in to "help" preserve it?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/stitching-the-future-technology-as-a-sacred-trust

**[Sweden — Lund University]** — An economic historian is redesigning how he assesses academic writing now that generative AI can produce a competent first draft in seconds, shifting the focus from the finished essay to visible evidence of the drafting and revision process. Rather than banning AI outright, the approach asks students to show their thinking at intermediate stages, making the writing process itself part of what's graded.
*For educators:* If a polished final draft no longer proves a student did the thinking, what evidence would actually convince you they did?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/assessing-students-academic-writing-when-ai-can-produce-first-draft

**[United States — K-12]** — A school principal and a teenage student independently tried out OpenAI's newly launched ChatGPT for Teens, comparing its built-in safety guardrails against how a real teenager actually uses the tool day to day. Their side-by-side reactions surfaced gaps between what the safety features are designed to catch and what teens actually ask the chatbot to do.
*For educators:* Whose judgment should decide if a "teen-safe" AI product is actually safe — the company that built the guardrails, or the students living inside them?
