# AI in Education Digest — 2026-07-09

**Global / UNESCO** — UNESCO warns the worldwide trade in fake degrees has grown into a roughly $22 billion industry, supercharged by generative AI tools that make forged transcripts and diplomas easier to produce than ever. Experts are now pushing for cryptographic verification and stronger cross-border credential-checking networks — meaning the same AI wave fueling the fraud may also be the best tool for stopping it.
*For educators:* If verifying a credential becomes as automatic as scanning a QR code, does that shift responsibility for catching fraud away from individual admissions officers and employers and onto the institutions that issue the credential in the first place?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/global-cooperation-needed-tackle-disease-fake-degrees

**United Kingdom — University of Surrey** — Surrey has rewritten every one of its degree programmes to embed discipline-specific AI use starting this September, applying the change even to students already partway through their courses. Assessment is shifting from grading final "outputs" to grading visible "process" — English literature students now submit annotated close-reading drafts and peer-feedback notes, while theatre students submit rehearsal journals documenting abandoned ideas and failed experiments.
*For educators:* When failed attempts and messy drafts become part of the graded record, how do you keep students honest about showing real struggle rather than performing a curated version of "productive failure" for the grader?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/surrey-redesigns-all-degrees-embed-discipline-specific-ai

**China — Greater Bay Area (Guangdong / Hong Kong / Macao)** — A study of 33 academics across the region found humanities lecturers were far more likely than their peers to quietly dock marks whenever they merely suspected — without proof — that a student had used AI. Tellingly, many of those same academics reversed their judgment once reminded that their own institution's policy explicitly permitted declared AI use, exposing how much grading still runs on gut suspicion rather than written rules.
*For educators:* If a lecturer's private bias against AI use is shaping grades even when policy allows it, what does that say about how much an official AI policy can actually control what happens inside a marker's head?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/academics-marking-students-down-when-they-suspect-ai-use
