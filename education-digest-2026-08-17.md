# AI in Education Digest — August 17, 2026

**United Kingdom** — A paper from the Staff and Educational Development Association, based on interviews with early-career academics at UK universities, found that generative AI has intensified confusion over what assessments are actually meant to test — leaving newer faculty feeling unsupported and unsure how to respond to AI-assisted work. Researchers concluded that AI didn't create these tensions so much as expose institutional support gaps that already existed.
*For educators:* If students and staff can't agree on what an assessment is actually testing, is AI the real problem — or is it the assessment design?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/students-confused-about-assessment-purpose-age-ai

**United States (Boston)** — At the America's Youth AI Festival, "student senators" from across the country debated and drafted the first student-authored national AI policy proposal for K-12 classrooms, including a rule against independent AI use before ninth grade. The event inverted the usual top-down dynamic, putting students rather than administrators in charge of setting the guardrails.
*For educators:* What would change in your classroom's AI rules if your students had written them instead of you?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/on-ai-policy-students-have-plenty-to-say

**United States** — A review coding AI policies from more than 100 school districts found that most guidance leans cautious, is often disconnected from state-level recommendations, and was written with little real data on what's actually happening in classrooms. The piece follows a district trying to translate broad policy language into rules a teacher can actually use day to day.
*For educators:* Does your school's AI policy reflect what's actually happening in classrooms, or what administrators assumed would happen?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/what-happens-when-ai-policy-meets-a-real-classroom
