# Education Digest — September 7, 2026

**[Hong Kong / Taiwan / South Korea — Higher Education]** — A controlled test by computer engineers at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology found that exam answers fed through AI-enabled smart glasses scored 92.5%, placing among the top five performers in an undergraduate exam, while Times Higher Education reports similar incidents surfacing across the region — a Taiwanese medical-school applicant caught via heat emitted from the frame, and cheating flagged in South Korean English-proficiency tests. Unlike phones or cheat sheets, the devices are discreet and hands-free, and researchers argue invigilators now have no reliable way to detect their use.

*For educators:* If a device indistinguishable from ordinary eyewear can feed exam answers undetected, does in-person invigilation still function as a meaningful safeguard?

**[United Kingdom — Higher Education]** — The London School of Innovation has become the first UK institution granted degree-awarding powers for a postgraduate model built entirely around AI-led teaching: every student is assigned a 24/7 AI "private tutor" that gives formative feedback and runs end-of-module Socratic dialogues to check understanding. Human academics still mark all summative assessments and students can request a human review of any AI-generated feedback, but day-to-day teaching contact is designed to run through the AI system rather than through lectures.

*For educators:* If formative feedback can run entirely through an always-available AI tutor, which parts of teaching would you insist stay human even in a fully AI-led course design?

Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/virtual-lecturers-be-overseen-academics-ai-university

**[United States — K-12]** — A nationally representative Gallup/Walton Family Foundation survey of more than 2,000 U.S. teachers found that 82% have received no formal guidance on applying AI to their work, with the largest gaps around one-on-one tutoring (69% unguided) and grading and feedback (58% unguided). EdSurge reports the gap is uneven by wealth: teachers in higher-needs schools are less likely than those in wealthier schools to receive any AI guidance at all, compounding an existing resource divide.

*For educators:* If your school has no formal AI guidance yet, which single task — grading, tutoring, or lesson planning — most urgently needs clear rules first?
