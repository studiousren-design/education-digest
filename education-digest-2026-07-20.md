# AI in Education Digest — 2026-07-20

**United States — K-12 ESL/bilingual instruction, Harvard research** — Teachers are experimenting with AI chatbots' built-in multilingual features for English-language learners — letting a student type a question in Spanish while the tool responds in English, or using AI to flag which vocabulary in a lesson will trip up bilingual students before class starts. Harvard researchers cited in the piece caution that many of these systems are still measurably worse at recognizing accented or dialect-inflected speech, so the students who could benefit most from voice-based AI support may currently get the least out of it.
*For educators:* If a tool works noticeably better for "standard" English speakers than for the accented or bilingual speakers it's marketed to help, is rolling it out now closing a gap for ESL students, or quietly opening a new one?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/ai-meets-esl-and-teachers-are-intrigued

**China — foreign-language departments at multiple universities** — Following Shenzhen University of Advanced Technology's early-July announcement that it will phase out standard English classes in favor of intercultural-communication courses (citing real-time AI translation as the reason language proficiency is no longer the barrier it was), language departments elsewhere are redesigning rather than closing: the Communication University of China has reworked its six-decade-old "Swahili Translation: Theory and Practice" course after students asked whether human translation is still worth learning, and Shanghai International Studies University has formed an "AI plus" steering group to rebuild its programs around auto-translation tools. Beijing Foreign Studies University is going the opposite direction, adding hybrid majors like "Japanese with Japan studies" that pair language with a second discipline.
*For educators:* If AI genuinely removes translation as a bottleneck, what should a language degree teach students to do with that freed-up time and attention — and who decides that, the department or the graduates competing for jobs?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/chinese-language-departments-redraw-courses-response-ai

**Hong Kong — Hong Kong Polytechnic University** — Two faculty members in the department of applied social sciences built a student-facing AI assistant embedded directly inside WhatsApp rather than as a standalone app, on the reasoning that support tools only work if they live where students already are instead of requiring them to open something new. The authors frame it as a step toward an "omnichannel" model where the same AI assistant follows a student across whichever platform they're already using.
*For educators:* When an AI assistant is folded into the messaging app students check dozens of times a day, does that make it more genuinely useful — or does it just make it harder for students to notice they're relying on AI at all, let alone opt out?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/case-omnichannel-ai-assistant
