# AI in Education Digest — 2026-07-31

**United Kingdom — University of Manchester** — Times Higher Education reports that Manchester became the first university in the world to promise blanket access to Microsoft's M365 Copilot for all 65,000 of its students and staff, with staff access already live and student access to follow next academic year; critics warn that rolling AI out to everyone at once, without course-specific guardrails, risks normalising uncritical use before a student advisory group's "co-created" guidance materials are even ready.
*For educators:* If every student in your course got Copilot access tomorrow with no discipline-specific guidance attached, what is the one assignment you'd need to redesign this week rather than next term?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/caution-urged-over-blanket-roll-out-ai-staff-and-students

**United States — The Fenn School, Massachusetts, and a New Jersey middle school** — EdSurge brought together two teachers on opposite sides of the same question: Nathan Kraai, director of innovation and design thinking at The Fenn School, uses a trip to a record store to argue that AI hasn't changed what people fundamentally want from learning, while Court Shuller, a middle school English teacher in New Jersey, is drawing a hard line around specific tasks he refuses to let AI touch regardless of the time it would save.
*For educators:* Which single task in your own classroom would you ban AI from outright, even knowing it would mean more work for your students?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/what-should-students-still-have-to-do-for-themselves

**United States — Ideal School, Tampa, Florida (bilingual K–12)** — EdSurge profiles teacher Daniel Gomez at this bilingual K-12 school, who is piloting AI chatbots that let emergent bilingual students reason in Spanish and English simultaneously to build a concept, treating the home language as a resource for understanding rather than an obstacle to clear before "real" learning can start.
*For educators:* If a chatbot let your multilingual students work through an idea in their first language before translating it into English, would you count that as a shortcut around the task, or as the task itself?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/ai-meets-esl-and-teachers-are-intrigued
