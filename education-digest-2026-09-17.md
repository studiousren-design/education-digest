# AI in Education Digest — September 17, 2026

**[India]** — A new Times Higher Education/D2L white paper argues India's higher education sector should treat AI as strategic infrastructure rather than a bolt-on tool, urging institutions to build faculty capacity and embed AI skills directly into curricula as the country's student population and digital ambitions both scale rapidly. The paper draws on perspectives from Indian higher-education leaders navigating a landscape reshaped simultaneously by AI, shifting student demand and policy reform.

*For educators:* If AI is "infrastructure" rather than a tool, does that change who in your institution should own decisions about it — IT, faculty, or someone else entirely?

Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/ai-indias-higher-education-future-teaching-and-learning

**[Global — research community]** — A survey of over 2,600 researchers worldwide by Oxford University Press found that while 64% say they've benefited from incorporating AI into their research, only 36% actually record how they used it, revealing a wide gap between AI adoption and transparent disclosure in scholarship. The finding raises questions about whether academic norms around citing and documenting AI assistance are keeping pace with how researchers are actually working.

*For educators:* If most researchers already use AI but few disclose it, what does that suggest about the disclosure rules you're asking your own students to follow?

Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/researchers-not-fully-disclosing-ai-use-oup-survey-reveals

**[Argentina / Colombia]** — At Universidad Austral's business school, faculty built generative AI directly into a five-week assessment cycle, having students use it as a collaborative "teaching colleague" across three assignments and a final presentation, then reviewing each student's conversation history with the tool to see exactly where it added value versus where the student's own thinking took over. Every group in the pilot said the arrangement improved their formative feedback loop compared to standard assessment.

*For educators:* If you could see a transcript of every student's conversation with AI on an assignment, would that change what you choose to grade?
