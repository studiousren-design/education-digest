# Education Digest — 2026-08-28

**[United Kingdom]** — A Staff and Educational Development Association survey of UK university educators found that early-career academics increasingly see students treating writing assignments as a product to hand in rather than a process for developing disciplinary thinking, a shift they link to how easily generative AI can produce a finished essay or report. Researchers say the confusion is widening the gap between what assessments are meant to measure and how students actually approach them, and are calling on institutions to make the purpose of writing more explicit rather than just tightening AI rules.

*For educators:* If a student can't say why an assignment exists beyond "to get a grade," is that a student problem or an assignment-design problem?

Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/students-confused-about-assessment-purpose-age-ai

**[United States]** — EdSurge's reporting traces AI spending in schools past the classroom, from the time teachers save using AI tools to grade and give feedback, to the electricity and infrastructure costs districts are only starting to reckon with as usage scales. The piece argues that most AI rollout conversations start with curriculum and instruction and stop there, leaving the actual invoice — and the growing power bill behind it — as an afterthought.

*For educators:* Before adopting a new AI tool for your classroom, do you know what it costs your district once the infrastructure behind it is counted, not just the license fee?

Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/what-is-ai-actually-costing-schools
