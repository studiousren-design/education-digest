# AI in Education Digest — September 8, 2026

**[United States]** — At July's America's Youth AI Festival in Boston, more than 90 student senators representing all 50 states drafted and passed the "STUDENTS FIRST Act," the nation's first student-authored national AI policy framework for K-12 schools, by an 82–16 vote. EdSurge reports that the students, not administrators, set the terms for acceptable classroom AI use, from academic integrity safeguards to data privacy protections.
*For educators:* If your students wrote your school's AI policy instead of your administration, what would change first?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/on-ai-policy-students-have-plenty-to-say

**[Global / Higher Education]** — An agentic AI tool marketed as "Einstein AI" offered to log into students' virtual learning environments, watch lectures, join discussions, and submit assignments on their behalf "while they slept," before being taken down after a trademark dispute rather than an ethics review. Times Higher Education notes the episode exposed how easily agentic AI connected to systems like Canvas or Blackboard could complete an entire course autonomously, with almost no institutional safeguards in place to detect it.
*For educators:* If an AI agent could pass your course start to finish without your students touching it, what would that reveal about how the course is assessed?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/strange-case-einstein-ai-spotlights-chatbot-concerns

**[Hong Kong, Indonesia, Japan, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, South Korea, Thailand]** — A report presented at THE's Asia Universities Summit found that universities across these eight systems already show high "digital maturity," but most lack the advanced infrastructure and coordinated investment needed to translate that into real AI capability in teaching and research. Times Higher Education reports the gap is less about access to AI tools and more about strategy, leadership, and funding coordination across institutions.
*For educators:* Is your institution's AI adoption limited more by access to tools or by a lack of coordinated strategy for using them well?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/more-coordinated-investment-needed-unlock-asian-ai-potential
