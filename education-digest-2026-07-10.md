# AI in Education Digest — 2026-07-10

**Mexico — Tecnológico de Monterrey** — Architecture and design faculty (Lesly Pliego, Fernanda Peña and Antonio Juárez) are running a studio sequence where students must hand-sketch and photograph their own design concepts before feeding those images into AI generators like Midjourney, Adobe Firefly or DALL-E to produce 3D-informed renders, which are then refined further using augmented reality overlays. The ordering is deliberate: sketch-first, prompt-second, so the AI amplifies a student's design intent rather than replacing it.
*For educators:* If the exercise's integrity depends on students sketching before prompting, how would you actually catch a student who generated the AI image first and reverse-engineered a "sketch" to match it?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/teaching-design-3d-blending-sketching-ai-and-ar

**Singapore — Nanyang Technological University** — Physics lecturer Gerard Joseph Lim is using agentic AI — tools that can plan, write code and iteratively refine their own output with minimal human input — to generate interactive STEM simulations on the fly, letting students manipulate parameters and watch outcomes change in real time instead of reading about them in a textbook.
*For educators:* If an agent can spin up a working simulation for almost any concept a student happens to ask about, what becomes of the small set of demonstrations a teacher has spent years carefully building and refining?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/how-agentic-ai-brings-interactivity-stem-learning

**United Kingdom — university staff survey** — Despite real redundancy waves already hitting UK universities, a Times Higher Education staff survey found most academic and professional-services employees don't personally expect AI to make them redundant within three years: 55% disagreed with the idea, and a quarter of STEM staff "strongly" disagreed compared with just 17% of humanities and arts staff.
*For educators:* If the people most exposed to AI's disruption of their own jobs are also the least worried about it, is that a sign they understand the risk better than outsiders — or that they're underestimating it?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/ai-risk-university-jobs-despite-staff-believing-roles-are-safe
