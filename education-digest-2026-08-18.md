# AI in Education Digest — August 18, 2026

**Mexico** — UNAM, Mexico's largest university, suspended processing of admissions results after AI-detection software flagged an anomalous spike in near-perfect entrance exam scores — 16.3% of takers scored 100 or above on its first-ever remote exam, versus a historical average of 3.5%. An AI specialist estimated up to half of the roughly 150,000 test-takers may have used tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude to cheat, and UNAM ordered around 58,000 applicants to retake the exam in person.
*For educators:* If a remote assessment can't survive contact with AI tools, is the fix better detection, or an assessment that doesn't rely on remote, unsupervised conditions in the first place?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/mexican-university-suspends-admissions-after-cheating-scandal

**United Kingdom** — The University of Leicester is giving all 21,000+ students and 4,000+ staff full access to Microsoft 365 Copilot as one of the UK's first Microsoft "Frontier" universities, embedding the tool across teaching, research, and administration. Critics quoted alongside the rollout warn that campus-wide, opt-out-by-default AI access risks normalizing uncritical use before adequate guardrails and training are in place.
*For educators:* Would you rather roll out an AI tool to everyone and build guardrails as issues surface, or hold back access until the guardrails exist first?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/caution-urged-over-blanket-roll-out-ai-staff-and-students

**Hong Kong** — Researchers at The Education University of Hong Kong built YoChatGPT!, a shared classroom chatroom where an entire class interacts with an AI model together rather than through private, individual chats, with an anonymous-posting option so students can flag bias or wrong answers without exposing themselves. The tool has also been used in classrooms in the Philippines and was designed to tackle AI literacy and student inclusion as a single problem rather than two separate ones.
*For educators:* Would your quieter students engage more with AI-assisted discussion if their questions and mistakes were visible to the class, but not to them personally?
