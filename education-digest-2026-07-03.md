# AI in Education Digest — 3 July 2026

**China** — A May 2026 survey of 70 Chinese universities found sweeping cuts to foreign-language and translation majors — eight universities dropped Japanese, five cut German, five cut translation studies — as automated translation tools erode demand for those degrees. Shanghai International Studies University has launched an "AI plus" steering group to redesign its offerings, while Beijing Foreign Studies University is folding language study into interdisciplinary majors like "Japanese with Japan studies," part of a wider wave in which Chinese universities have revoked or suspended over 12,200 undergraduate programmes since 2021 while launching 10,200 new ones, many in AI and robotics.
*For educators:* If a skill your discipline teaches is being displaced by AI tools rather than complemented by them, would you fold it into a broader interdisciplinary major, redesign it around what AI still can't do, or let it wind down — and how would you decide?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/chinese-language-departments-redraw-courses-response-ai

**UK** — London School of Innovation has become one of the first UK providers granted full degree-awarding powers for an explicitly "AI-led" teaching model, enrolling its first postgraduate cohort in June 2026. Students are assigned AI "private tutors" — delivered as text or through an AI avatar — that lead personalised modules and run Socratic-dialogue sessions to check understanding, while human academics oversee content, hold one-to-one consultations, and mark all summative assessments themselves.
*For educators:* If an AI tutor handled the day-to-day instruction and formative checks in your course, which moments would you insist stay human — and would "Socratic dialogue" with a bot actually build the same understanding as one with a person?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/virtual-lecturers-be-overseen-academics-ai-university

**US** — A new CoSN "State of EdTech" report finds 79% of US school districts now have formal AI guidelines, up from 57% in 2025, and districts running AI-focused operational initiatives jumped from 37% to 64% year over year — yet 98% of school IT officials say AI is opening new cyberattack vectors faster than budgets and staff expertise can keep up. The most common district AI initiative right now isn't a classroom tool at all: training staff to use instruction-focused generative AI, something seven in ten districts are now doing.
*For educators:* Your district likely has an AI policy now — but does anyone in your building actually know what it says, and would you recognize a legitimate AI tool from a spoofed one if it showed up in your inbox?
