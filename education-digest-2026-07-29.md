# AI in Education Digest — 2026-07-29

**United States — public university in Texas, 31,692 course syllabi analysed** — Times Higher Education reports on research by UC Berkeley's Igor Chirikov, who used a large language model to scan five years of syllabi (2021–2025) at a large Texas public university and found faculty easing off outright AI bans in every discipline except the arts and humanities; syllabi mentioning academic-integrity concerns fell from 63% in spring 2023 to 49% by autumn 2025, while those requiring students to attribute AI use jumped from 1% to 29% over the same period.
*For educators:* If your own policy still reads as a blanket ban, does the data suggest students and colleagues have already moved past it — and what would a task-by-task policy (banning AI for drafting but allowing it for coding, say) look like in your course?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/academics-moving-away-outright-bans-ai-study-finds

**United Arab Emirates — Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence, Abu Dhabi** — Times Higher Education profiles MBZUAI, a government-funded graduate research university founded in 2019 that has since hired more than 100 faculty from China, the US, Germany and elsewhere, offers full scholarships and grant-free research positions, and now ranks among the world's top 20 universities for AI publications — part of a wider Gulf push, alongside Saudi Arabia, to build research capacity as a hedge against oil dependence.
*For educators:* When a government builds a university from scratch around a single technology and removes the usual grant-chasing pressure from faculty, what does that change about the kind of research — and teaching — that gets produced?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/middle-east-bets-ai-research-strengthen-its-universities

**United States — Eedi Labs, K-12 math tutoring** — EdSurge covers efforts by Eedi and other developers to train AI not just to solve math problems but to predict the specific misconception behind a wrong answer, using a "human in the loop" model where tutors review and edit AI-drafted messages before students see them, aiming to give teachers real-time diagnostic detail on where a student's thinking broke down.
*For educators:* Catching *why* a student got a problem wrong is harder than checking *that* they got it wrong — would you trust an AI's diagnosis of a misconception enough to reteach based on it, or only use it as a prompt to look closer yourself?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/2025-12-11-teaching-machines-to-spot-human-errors-in-math-assignments
