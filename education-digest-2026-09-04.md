# Education Digest — September 4, 2026

**[United Kingdom — Higher Education]** — At Birmingham City University, a marketing lecturer built an AI assistant deliberately restricted to a fixed set of trusted course materials — lecture slides, rubrics, reading lists — so students can query it in different languages without risking hallucinated or off-syllabus answers. After piloting the approach on one module, the awarding gap between student groups narrowed from 21.4% to 18.2%, early evidence that walling AI off from the open internet can support equity, not just convenience.

*For educators:* Would restricting your course's AI assistant to only your own materials trade away its versatility for a meaningful gain in trust and fairness?

**[United States — Higher Education]** — Instructors at the University of Florida built a five-part AI literacy framework — knowing, using, evaluating and creating AI — directly into course design rather than bolting it on as a one-off orientation session, opening each semester with an explicit discussion of what AI is, how it works and where its biases lie. Students are then asked to evaluate AI-generated output for bias or inaccuracy as a recurring assignment type, not a special add-on.

*For educators:* If AI literacy became a graded, recurring skill in your syllabus rather than a single orientation session, what would you cut to make room for it?

Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/make-ai-literacy-foundational-element-your-course-design

**[Higher Education — Course Design]** — A THE Campus case argues AI-era course design needs three shifts, with instructors moving from primary knowledge-transmitters to coaches; one example embeds real industry problems — a regulatory change, a sustainability issue — into a module, with outside experts giving feedback while students research with AI, weigh incomplete evidence and present under genuine uncertainty. The pitch is deliberately small-scale: pick one existing assignment this semester and introduce a single layer of supported ambiguity rather than redesigning an entire course at once.

*For educators:* Which one assignment in your current course could absorb a dose of real, unresolved ambiguity without overwhelming students who are still building confidence?
