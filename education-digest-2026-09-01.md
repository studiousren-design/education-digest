# Education Digest — September 1, 2026

**[United Kingdom — University of Southampton]** — The Russell Group university announced it will not renew its Turnitin contract beyond the 2026–27 academic year after the company proposed changes that would let it use students' submitted work to train its AI models. It's a rare case of an institution walking away from the anti-plagiarism tool it relies on precisely because of how that tool wants to use AI.

*For educators:* If the software you use to police AI-assisted cheating is itself becoming an AI company, what should that change about how you vet the fine print of any ed-tech contract?

Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/southampton-dumps-turnitin-over-use-students-work-train-ai

---

**[United Kingdom — University of Surrey]** — This month, Surrey becomes one of the first UK universities to embed discipline-specific AI instruction into every degree program rather than offering a single generic "AI literacy" module, redesigning assessments to weigh students' reasoning process over their final output. The change applies even to students partway through their degrees, not just new entrants.

*For educators:* If "AI literacy" has to look different in an engineering lab than in a nursing placement, what would teaching AI "through your discipline" actually require you to rebuild in your own course?

Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/surrey-redesigns-all-degrees-embed-discipline-specific-ai

---

**[United States, United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand]** — New comparative data cited by EdSurge found that U.S. higher-ed students submit AI-generated writing at roughly double the rate of peers in the UK and Australia (10% each), while a New Zealand high school teacher described how the country's centralized ministry structure is shaping AI rollout differently than Australia's state-by-state approach.

*For educators:* If national or state policy structure this strongly predicts how fast AI reaches classrooms, what would it take your own system to move faster — or is slower actually safer here?

Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/teachers-forge-ahead-on-integrating-ai
