# AI in Education Digest — September 14, 2026

**[United States — K-12 Special Education]** — Parents are increasingly bringing AI-generated summaries, questions, and even drafted goals into IEP meetings, using chatbots to decode dense special-education jargon and prepare before sitting down with school staff. Teachers are having to rethink these meetings as a collaborative negotiation rather than a one-way briefing, since parents now sometimes arrive more technically fluent — or more primed to push back — than before.
*For educators:* If a parent's AI-drafted questions are sharper than the ones your team prepared for, whose expertise is actually driving the IEP?

**[Malaysia — Higher Education]** — The Asian Women's Leadership University College, a small nonprofit liberal-arts college in Penang now 15 months old, is explicitly building its curriculum around critical thinking and problem-solving because, as its leadership puts it, "with what AI is doing," those skills are what will matter most. Its first cohort — a master's program in leadership development — draws students from Malaysia, Bangladesh, Ethiopia, and Tanzania into a humanities-sciences-maths blend designed to outlast whatever AI can automate.
*For educators:* If a brand-new institution can build its whole pitch around "AI made this necessary," what would it take for an established program to make the same case?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/malaysian-female-liberal-arts-college-addresses-critical-gaps

**[United Kingdom — Higher Education]** — A Henley Business School lecturer argues that checking an AI answer for accuracy misses the real risk: a factually correct response can still smuggle in assumptions about whose situation is "default" and which options never get mentioned. She proposes four low-cost classroom exercises — needing no new tools or budget — that train students to interrogate a fluent answer for whose perspective it quietly assumes, not just whether it's true.
*For educators:* When was the last time you asked a class not "is this AI answer correct?" but "whose situation is it assuming is normal?"
