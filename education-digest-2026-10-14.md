# AI in Education Digest — 14 October 2026

> Note: few items were published in the last 48 hours in search results. Only story 1 is confirmed as 13 October; story 2 was reported around 9–10 October and story 3's date could not be verified.

**[Africa / Pan-African research network]** — Scholars behind the Deep Learning Indaba, which grew from a group of eight people hoping to find 30 others into a network of tens of thousands, say African-produced AI research can "push the boundaries" of the field. Benjamin Rosman of the University of the Witwatersrand notes that postgraduates once had to go abroad to study AI, but can now increasingly train on the continent.
*For educators:* If your students can now study AI where they live, whose problems and languages should shape the questions they are taught to ask?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/africa-has-crucial-role-advancing-study-ai-say-professors

**[United Kingdom / Universities]** — Newcastle and Westminster have joined Southampton in dropping Turnitin, with Newcastle citing licence changes that could allow student submissions to be processed with AI tools, and Westminster saying the tool is largely ineffective against generative AI. Turnitin has delayed the licence changes until at least September 2027 while students' unions and universities such as Cambridge push back.
*For educators:* Who should own the data in a student's essay, and what would you give up if you stopped using the detector?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/more-universities-end-turnitin-contracts-concerns-mount

**[United Kingdom / Higher education assessment]** — Academics warn that universities racing to make assessment "AI-proof" risk over-assessing students, piling on exams and tasks that could drive burnout. 
*For educators:* If you added one assessment because of AI, which existing one could you remove?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/universities-risk-over-assessing-response-ai
