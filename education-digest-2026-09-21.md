# Education Digest — September 21, 2026

**Hong Kong** — Hong Kong's government has fast-tracked plans for three new "university towns" in its Northern Metropolis, with the first, San Tin, built around artificial intelligence, robotics and medicine; 19 higher-education institutions have already expressed interest in establishing a presence there. Universities are cautiously optimistic about the 2027/28 timeline but warn that ambition needs to be matched with real public investment.

*For educators:* If your institution had to design a physical campus — not just a lab or an elective — entirely around AI as a discipline, what would you put at its center, and what would you deliberately leave out?

**United States (Oklahoma & Alabama)** — An elementary school principal and a high school junior test-drove OpenAI's new ChatGPT for Teens together, finding its built-in guardrails nudged students toward critical thinking rather than simple answers — but both agreed the tool assumes a level of AI fluency most parents and teachers don't yet have. Their side-by-side review is a rare case of adult and student voices assessing the same new product in real time.

*For educators:* If a "teen-safe" AI tool already knows more about effective prompting than most of the adults supervising its use, who should actually be setting the norms for it in your classroom?

Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/a-principal-and-a-student-reviewed-the-new-chatgpt-for-teens-they-had-plenty-to-say

**United States (Tribal/Indigenous education)** — An instructional technology coordinator at a Native-serving school recounts fielding a 2021 pitch to train a "virtual ancestor" AI on elders' recorded voices and stories, and turns that experience into a framework for vetting any edtech: who owns the data, does it respect community sovereignty, and does it bridge relationships or replace them. The piece reframes AI adoption in schools as a question of stewardship rather than novelty.

*For educators:* Before adopting an AI tool trained on your students' or community's own material, could you clearly answer who owns the data it runs on?

Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/stitching-the-future-technology-as-a-sacred-trust
