# AI in Education Digest — 2026-10-13

> Note: search tools returned no EdSurge or Times Higher Education stories verifiably published in the last 48 hours, and no stories from non-English-speaking countries. The three items below are the most recent uncovered AI-in-education stories found (roughly 2 weeks old, per search-result dates).

**[United States / K-12 edtech vendors]** — An EdReports brief found that curriculum and edtech providers are rapidly embedding AI features into instructional materials with little proof they improve learning. Only one of the 10 vendors studied supplied third-party evidence of better outcomes.
*For educators:* If a vendor can't show independent evidence that an AI feature helps students learn, what would you need to see before letting it into your classroom?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/study-edtech-is-rushing-ai-integration-before-proving-it-works

**[United States / School districts]** — An EdSurge podcast episode (Sept. 30) with EdReports' chief academic officer discusses how districts can vet AI features in edtech products. The traditional line between core and supplemental materials is blurring as AI is added to both.
*For educators:* Who in your school or district decides whether an AI feature is trustworthy, and what criteria do they use?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/can-schools-trust-ai-features-in-edtech-products

**[Global / Universities and OpenAI]** — OpenAI has launched an inaugural student ambassador programme across campuses worldwide, prompting concern that AI is deepening its foothold in higher education. Commentators say universities have little choice but to be complicit.
*For educators:* When a vendor recruits your students as ambassadors, who is shaping the norms of AI use on your campus: you or the vendor?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/openai-campus-little-choice-be-complicit
