# AI in Education Digest — 2026-10-08

**[Denmark / University of Copenhagen]** — Denmark's largest university has named anthropologist Morten Axel Pedersen to the newly created post of deputy prorector for AI, rather than hiring a computer scientist. He argues that leaders have little idea how people actually use AI day to day, so the university's strategy starts by studying real use, which he sees as an anthropological question.
*For educators:* Do you know how your students and colleagues actually use AI, or only what they say they do when asked?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/copenhagen-university-turns-anthropologist-lead-ai-strategy

**[Sweden / Global research publishing]** — Swedish AI start-up Redpine will get access to more than 20 million research papers from six publishers, including Sage, the BMJ group and China's Wanfang Data. Publishers set what an AI agent may use and the price per use, and authors are paid each time their work is accessed.
*For educators:* If AI agents can read paywalled research and pay authors per use, how should we teach students to cite and evaluate what those agents retrieve?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/publishers-share-20-million-research-papers-ai-start

**[United Arab Emirates / Higher education]** — UAE university enrolments rose about 15% in 2025-26 to more than 218,000 students, with diploma and higher diploma enrolments up 173% since 2021-22. Experts credit the growth partly to new AI-focused courses and to Western countries becoming less welcoming to international students, though the article does not isolate a cause.
*For educators:* If shorter, AI-focused credentials draw the most growth, what should a full degree offer that they do not?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/uae-big-winner-wests-anti-internationalisation-turn
