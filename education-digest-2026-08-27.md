# Education Digest — 2026-08-27

**[United Kingdom — Higher Education]** — A study published in the journal *Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education* found that AI tools such as ChatGPT typically award higher marks than human markers when grading undergraduate essays, with gaps as wide as 40 points on a 100-point scale in individual cases. The researchers found AI systems tended to inflate marks for weaker essays while grading strong essays more harshly than humans did, aligning most closely with human judgment only in the middle of the scoring range.

*For educators:* If an AI grader is most trustworthy exactly where the stakes are lowest — the safely average essay — what does that tell you about handing it any borderline grade?

**[United States]** — EdSurge looks at how educators are working to safeguard students' capacity for independent thought as AI use grows, pointing to practices like handwritten work, face-to-face discussion, and problem-solving before AI assistance is introduced as ways to preserve real thinking time. The piece frames these choices as a deliberate counterweight to "cognitive offloading," where reaching for an AI tool too early can substitute for the mental effort that actually builds understanding.

*For educators:* Which of your routine assignments could you redesign as a deliberately AI-free first pass — and would you be willing to grade it more slowly in exchange?
