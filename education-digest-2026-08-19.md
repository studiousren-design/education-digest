# AI in Education Digest — August 19, 2026

**United Kingdom** — Think tank Policy Exchange used freedom-of-information data from 120 UK universities to find that 78% still rely on unsupervised remote exams, and is urging the Office for Students and the QAA to ban the format "immediately," arguing it actively invites AI-assisted cheating while punishing honest students relative to peers who use it. The report frames the issue as a systemic assessment-design failure rather than a student-integrity problem.
*For educators:* If unsupervised remote assessment can no longer be trusted to measure a student's own work, what happens to the courses and skills that were only ever assessed that way?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/ban-all-remote-unsupervised-tests-immediately-urges-report

**United Kingdom** — Times Higher Education asked seven universities running major AI rollouts to disclose the carbon and water footprint of their AI use; four, including Oxford and Cambridge, did not respond, but the University of Surrey confirmed it has rewritten its procurement policy to require AI suppliers to report carbon data, and MIT is expanding a machine-learning pilot that optimizes campus heating and cooling systems. The piece exposes a widening gap between universities' public AI ambitions and their willingness to account for AI's environmental cost.
*For educators:* Would your own institution's AI rollout survive the same carbon-disclosure request THE just sent to Oxford and Cambridge?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/depth/universities-ai-embrace-undermining-their-net-zero-targets
