# AI in Education Digest — 16 October 2026

> **Freshness note:** Search results surfaced only one story that appears to fall in the last 48 hours (the Dutch theses story). The publishers' sites were not directly reachable from this environment, so the other two are the most specific *uncovered* stories found, with older dates stated below.

**[Netherlands / Higher education]** — Dutch broadcaster RTL Nieuws ran the Pangram detector over hundreds of recent theses from 12 universities and reported that in about a fifth, chatbot output outweighed the student's own contribution; education minister Rianne Letschert called AI-written theses "fraud". Universiteiten van Nederland, representing 14 universities, replied that a detector score alone cannot establish fraud and that the findings raise questions about how final assignments are designed.
*For educators:* If a detector score can't prove misconduct but a thesis may still not reflect the student's own thinking, what in your assessment design would make that question answerable?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/dutch-minister-criticises-fraud-fifth-theses-ai-written

**[United Kingdom / AI-taught postgraduate courses]** — The London School of Innovation, a postgraduate institution teaching machine learning, digital innovation and business transformation, was granted degree-awarding powers by the Office for Students (Times Higher Education, March 2026). Students get AI private tutors and a choice of written content or an AI avatar presenter, AI gives feedback on formative work, and staff shift to one-to-one mentoring while academics still mark summative assessments.
*For educators:* If AI handles explanation and formative feedback, what is the part of your role that students would still miss if it disappeared?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/virtual-lecturers-be-overseen-academics-ai-university

**[European Union / University compliance]** — Thomas Jørgensen of the European University Association warned that informal AI uses, such as academics using ChatGPT to assess student work, could breach the EU AI Act as its remaining provisions phase in (Times Higher Education, roughly April 2026). He said assessment use brings obligations for users and providers, that large general-purpose models may not meet transparency and training-data criteria, and that the legal position awaits guidance from the European Commission's AI Office.
*For educators:* Would you know today which of your colleagues use AI to help grade, and what would you do if that turned out to be non-compliant?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/looming-eu-ai-act-could-force-universities-change-everything
