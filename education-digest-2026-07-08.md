# AI in Education Digest — July 8, 2026

**United States / United Kingdom / Australia — higher education** — New Turnitin detection data shows 19.4% of US higher-ed students submit work that's more than 80% AI-generated, roughly double the rate seen in Australia (10.2%) and the UK (9.8%), with about half of all assessed American coursework showing at least some AI involvement. The gap between university and school-level writing is stark too, suggesting AI use accelerates sharply once students hit university-level workloads and stakes.
*For educators:* If AI-assisted writing is already the norm rather than the exception in your classroom, what does "original work" need to mean going forward?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/ai-usage-higher-among-american-students-finds-turnitin

**United Kingdom — research & teaching infrastructure** — A House of Commons select committee has warned that UK scientists and universities could be cut off "at a whim" from foreign-owned AI, quantum and space technologies because the country lacks its own sovereign capacity in these fields. MPs are calling for an overarching government plan to reduce dependence on other nations' AI systems for both research and teaching infrastructure.
*For educators:* How much of your institution's AI-dependent teaching and research runs on tools you don't actually control — and what's the contingency if access disappears overnight?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/uk-scientists-risk-losing-ai-access-whim

**Global higher education — research funding** — Experts are warning that autonomous AI agents capable of independently drafting and submitting grant applications could flood research-funding competitions, making it far harder for reviewers to tell genuinely strong proposals from AI-generated volume. Funders may need entirely new vetting processes as the same agentic tools reshaping coursework now start targeting the mechanisms that fund research itself.
*For educators:* If an AI agent can already write a competitive grant application, what uniquely human judgment are we still grading for in a research proposal?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/new-ai-agents-pose-existential-threat-how-grants-are-awarded
