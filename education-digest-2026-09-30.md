# AI in Education Digest — 30 September 2026

**[United States / K-12 curriculum and edtech]** — A new EdReports brief finds that curriculum and edtech providers are rapidly embedding AI features in instructional materials with little evidence they help learning. Only one of the 10 vendors studied offered third-party evidence that its AI add-ons improved student outcomes.
*For educators:* If your school's core curriculum vendor added AI features this year, what evidence did you ask to see before switching them on?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/study-edtech-is-rushing-ai-integration-before-proving-it-works

**[Australia and UK / Higher education]** — OpenAI has selected two undergraduates per campus as "OpenAI campus leads" who run workshops and weekly co-working sessions to promote its tools to peers. UNSW Sydney's Deborah Lupton calls it "incredibly unethical" and "exploitative", while Newcastle's Lilian Edwards says universities have "little choice but to be complicit".
*For educators:* When an AI company recruits your students as peer champions, who is accountable for the advice those students give?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/openai-campus-little-choice-be-complicit

**[United Kingdom / University-wide AI roll-outs]** — Manchester has promised M365 Copilot to its 65,000 students and staff, and Leicester is extending access across its community, while Liverpool is running a cautious proof-of-concept trial until November. Critics warn that blanket roll-outs imply everyone should use generative AI without showing it works in specific disciplines.
*For educators:* Would your department choose a campus-wide AI licence, or a discipline-by-discipline trial with evidence gathered first?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/caution-urged-over-blanket-roll-out-ai-staff-and-students
