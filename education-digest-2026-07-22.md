# AI in Education Digest — 2026-07-22

**United States — K-12 public schools** — A new Gallup/Walton Family Foundation study of 2,069 public school teachers ("Teaching for Tomorrow: Closing the Expectations Gap") finds only 18% have received any formal administrator guidance on AI use, with a third getting none at all, even as three-quarters of districts now have some form of AI policy on the books. ISTE+ASCD's Chief Innovation Officer Joseph South attributes the gap to AI being absent from teacher-prep programs and administrators hesitant to lead on a technology they don't yet understand themselves.
*For educators:* If your district has an AI policy but you've never been walked through it, is that a training gap or a signal the policy was written for compliance, not classroom use?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/ai-is-in-schools-teachers-are-not-ready

**United States — school district budgeting** — Unlike traditional software that gets cheaper to distribute at scale, generative AI keeps generating "inference costs" every time a user sends a prompt, leaving districts unable to predict what an AI-first rollout will actually cost once free tiers end. Concerns over student data flowing into commercial AI systems are pushing some districts toward private or locally hosted models, which raises the price further just as budgets are being locked in.
*For educators:* If your favorite AI tool tripled its price tomorrow, does your classroom practice survive the switch to a cheaper alternative — or was it built around one vendor's interface?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/can-schools-afford-an-ai-first-future

**United States — higher education governance** — At a roundtable held with Studiosity during the 2026 IHE US AI Summit, faculty described feeling forced into being "the auditor and policeman in the room" for AI use instead of teaching and assessing, while Studiosity's Jared Steuernol warned that ungoverned AI use risks turning students into "dependent operators rather than independent thinkers." UMass Dartmouth's Iren Valova countered that professors who ban AI outright should explain their reasoning to students, and described the university's move to a baseline AI-literacy curriculum with discipline-specific rules layered on top.
*For educators:* Could you explain, out loud and in one sentence, why AI is banned or allowed in your specific assignment — and would that reasoning survive a student pushing back on it?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/enabling-responsible-ai-adoption-through-governance
