# Education Digest — September 28, 2026

**[United Kingdom — Research funding]** — UK Research and Innovation used AI models to run an "initial triage" of 179 proposals in a pilot grant call, rejecting roughly half before any human reviewer saw them, judged against criteria like relevance, novelty, and feasibility. Academics are alarmed that a machine judgment, rather than a peer, may now end a promising research idea before it gets a human hearing.
*For educators:* If AI increasingly decides what research (or student work) gets a chance, how do we protect space for genuinely novel ideas that don't fit the patterns a model was trained to reward?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/ai-only-research-grant-rejection-ukri-funded-call-sparks-alarm

**[United States — Early childhood classrooms]** — Early childhood teachers are becoming reluctant media critics as "baby slop" — AI-generated YouTube videos aimed at toddlers and preschoolers — floods children's feeds; one channel alone posted nearly 5,500 such videos in under a year, some depicting physically impossible scenes. Educators are now coaching young children and families to question this content rather than absorb it uncritically.
*For educators:* At what age should critically evaluating AI-generated media become a formal part of the curriculum, rather than something addressed only after a problem shows up in class?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/ai-videos-are-warping-early-learning-teachers-say-heres-how-theyre-responding

**[United States — K-12 policy and training]** — A new Gallup/Walton Family Foundation survey finds that while three-quarters of school districts now have AI guidelines, 82 percent of teachers say they've never received formal guidance on how to use AI in their own work. The gap suggests district policy is moving faster than the professional development teachers actually need to act on it.
*For educators:* Given limited time and budget, is it more valuable right now to write clearer AI policy or to invest directly in teacher training — and can a district realistically do both at once?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/ai-is-in-schools-teachers-are-not-ready
