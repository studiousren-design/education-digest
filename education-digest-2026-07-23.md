# AI in Education Digest — 2026-07-23

**Indonesia — Jakarta, K-12 international school** — David Webb, a classroom teacher with no prior coding background, spent a year "vibe coding" an AI-powered book-recommendation app called LibraryAid that tracks roughly 30 factors per student — interests, reading history, classroom topics — to suggest personalized titles; one student reading two grade levels behind tripled his average reading progress after being matched to a book series he loved.
*For educators:* If a teacher with zero programming experience can vibe-code a working recommendation engine in a year, what's actually stopping more classrooms from building bespoke tools instead of buying off-the-shelf ones?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/vibe-coding-sparked-reading-interest-in-my-students

**United States — Georgia Tech, Atlanta** — Georgia Tech's College of Lifetime Learning is redesigning coursework around "disciplinary fluency" rather than generic AI skills, opening its AI Makerspace to the entire student body across all eight engineering disciplines and requiring students to draft and critique their own AI-assisted work specifically to surface hallucinations and nuances that only show up once AI output meets deep field context.
*For educators:* Would asking students to hunt for the hallucinations in their own AI-assisted drafts teach more disciplinary judgment than banning AI from the assignment entirely?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/how-integrate-aienabled-lifelong-learning-across-disciplines

**United Kingdom — national AI research base** — A new Elsevier analysis found 26.7% of UK AI-research papers rank in the global top 10% by citation impact (versus an 18.2% world average), with more than half of UK AI output co-authored internationally; the finding is prompting warnings that keeping this edge depends on deepening, not retreating from, cross-border collaboration even as UKRI commits over £1.6bn to the field.
*For educators:* If your institution's AI strategy is measured only in dollars spent or policies published, what would it look like to measure it in international co-authorships or shared curricula instead?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/collaborate-globally-retain-strength-ai-research-uk-urged
