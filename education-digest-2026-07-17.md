# Education Digest — 2026-07-17

**[Hong Kong]** — At the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, a teacher-training course now layers AI directly into its structure: chatbots field routine administrative questions from participants, while structured prompts guide trainee educators through drafting learning outcomes and designing activities. Trainees are explicitly required to question and refine the AI's output rather than accept it outright, so the course itself models the kind of AI use it wants new teachers to bring into their own classrooms.
*For educators:* If your own professional development modeled the exact relationship with AI you want to see in the classroom, what would have to change first — the content, or the delivery?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/when-teacher-training-mirrors-classroom-we-want-build

**[United States — Indian Community School]** — An Instructional Technology Coordinator at this Indigenous-serving school describes forming a cross-functional "Future Ready Team" of teachers, IT staff, and administrators to work out how the school would handle AI — through shared conversation and common-sense guidelines rather than a top-down ban, so the tech office became a trusted resource instead of an enforcement arm.
*For educators:* Who currently owns AI policy decisions at your school, and would a cross-functional team change what gets decided?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/how-my-school-used-common-sense-and-collaboration-to-confront-ai
