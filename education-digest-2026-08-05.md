# AI in Education Digest — 2026-08-05

**Mexico — UNAM suspends admissions after AI-monitored exam scandal** — Times Higher Education reports that UNAM, Mexico's top university, suspended new undergraduate enrollment after its AI-based exam-surveillance system flagged an unusually high share of perfect scores among roughly 150,000 entrance-exam candidates, leading to about 3,000 annulled tests. All applicants, including those not suspected of cheating, now face retaking the exam while a technical commission investigates and decides whether to validate or rerun the results.
*For educators:* When the AI tool built to catch cheating becomes the center of a fairness controversy itself, how do you decide when to trust an AI integrity system's verdict over a student's word?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/mexican-university-suspends-admissions-after-cheating-scandal

**United States — teachers, not IT leaders, are driving AI adoption** — EdSurge covers a new Turnitin "Learning Integrity Insights" report finding that 48% of surveyed educators say teachers and administrators — not technology leaders (17%) or cross-functional committees (16%) — hold primary responsibility for how AI gets implemented in their schools and districts. NCTE president Antero Garcia called the finding "heartening," framing grassroots teacher ownership as a healthier path than top-down mandates.
*For educators:* If teachers are becoming the default owners of AI policy by circumstance rather than design, what support do you most need to make good calls without formal guidance from above?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/teachers-forge-ahead-on-integrating-ai
