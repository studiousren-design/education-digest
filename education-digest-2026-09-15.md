# AI in Education Digest — September 15, 2026

**[Latin America — Higher Education]** — A UNESCO survey of 200 higher education institutions across 19 Latin American countries finds AI adoption "neither uniform nor evenly distributed": 87% of institutions use AI in at least one area, yet only 26% have a formal governance framework, and 55% have no dedicated AI budget at all — a gap that widens further at public universities, where just 4% have specific AI funding and 17% offer no staff training on it.
*For educators:* If nearly everyone at your institution is already using AI tools but almost no one has a budget or policy behind them, who is actually setting the rules?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/latin-america-must-address-uneven-ai-adoption-across-region

**[United States — K-8 Public Schools]** — Within the first three days of September, New York City and Los Angeles — the nation's two largest school districts — each imposed a moratorium on student-facing generative AI, together affecting well over a million students; NYC is pausing use for grades 2-K through 8 for a year and disabling AI features in 38 previously approved programs, while LA is restricting AI on all student-issued devices across every grade for the entire 2026-27 school year.
*For educators:* If the two biggest districts in the country are hitting pause rather than pushing forward, what would it take for your school to feel confident doing the opposite?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/new-york-and-los-angeles-ban-student-facing-ai-for-now-should-other-schools-follow

**[United States — Boston, K-12 Arts Education]** — At the "Me, Myself, and AI" art show held during the America's Youth AI Festival, judges reviewed work from the graduating class of 2026 — the first cohort with full exposure to generative AI tools throughout their schooling — and found students were notably deliberate about the line between using AI to strengthen a creative idea and letting it replace their own.
*For educators:* Could you tell, looking at a piece of student work, exactly where a student decided AI's suggestion wasn't worth taking?
