# Education Digest — September 25, 2026

**Latin America & the Caribbean (19 countries, higher education)** — A new UNESCO Observatory report finds AI use is now nearly universal in the region's universities (87% report some use), but governance hasn't kept pace: only about a quarter of institutions have a formal AI policy, and 55% have no dedicated AI budget at all, with public universities lagging far behind private ones on staff training. The report calls for urgent investment in governance frameworks and digital literacy so AI adoption doesn't widen the gap between well-resourced and under-resourced institutions.

*For educators:* If your institution's AI use is already outpacing its policies, what's the first governance gap you'd close before the next tool rolls out?

**Global (machine-learning academic publishing)** — Nihar Shah, editor-in-chief of the journal Transactions on Machine Learning Research, found that the share of submissions rejected without review has climbed from 6% in 2023 to 53% today; when he interviewed authors of desk-rejected, AI-assisted papers, three of seven couldn't answer basic questions about their own work. The journal is now testing annual submission quotas and parallel AI-generated reviews to cope with a flood of AI-assisted, lightly-understood manuscripts.

*For educators:* If a student — or a researcher — can't explain the reasoning behind their AI-assisted work, does that reveal more about the tool or about how the work was assigned?

Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/researchers-submitting-ai-assisted-papers-they-dont-understand

**Germany (open science policy)** — Kiel University's Julia Priess-Buchheit, who co-authored a study presented to the European Parliament in June, argues that generative AI's fast, probabilistic approach to knowledge sits uneasily with open science's emphasis on provenance, reproducibility, and licensing. She warns the mismatch risks attribution gaps, hallucinated citations, and license blindness as researchers lean more on AI tools for literature synthesis and writing.

*For educators:* Are you teaching students to scrutinize an AI output's sourcing and provenance as closely as they would a published citation?
