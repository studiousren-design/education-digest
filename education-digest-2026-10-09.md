# Education Digest — 2026-10-09

**[United Kingdom / Research funding]** — CRANE, a UKRI-backed cybersecurity research network hosted at Oxford, used several AI models to screen 179 grant proposals against seven criteria, and almost half were rejected without a human reading them. After academics objected, CRANE apologised and agreed to re-review every AI-screened rejection and reconvene its funding panel.
*For educators:* If an AI system filters student work or applications before a human sees it, what should students be told in advance, and who is accountable for the ones it screens out?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/745326

**[United States / New Mexico early literacy]** — New Mexico requires the Amira AI tool, which listens to children read aloud, to screen K–2 pupils for reading difficulties including dyslexia. Parents and lawmakers have raised data-privacy concerns and a number of districts have opted out, while an EdSurge opinion column argues that blanket anti-AI bans risk costing children early literacy help.
*For educators:* How should a school tell apart AI tools that talk back to children from AI tools that listen and flag, and should the two be governed by different rules?
Source: https://edsurge.com/news/will-kids-pay-the-price-for-ai-panic

**[Finland / University of Eastern Finland]** — Researchers surveyed 891 students and found that those who used AI instrumentally, to get answers, tended to earn lower grades than non-users. Those who used it to seek feedback on their work were linked to better performance.
*For educators:* Could your assignment design and AI guidance steer students towards asking for feedback rather than asking for answers?
Source: https://phys.org/news/2026-10-ai-feedback-ready-students-higher.html
