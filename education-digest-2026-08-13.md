# AI in Education Digest — 2026-08-13

**United States — Cheat Vulnerability Index (teacher-built tool)** — A teacher who built an AI grading tool turned the lesson around, creating the Cheat Vulnerability Index, a free web app where educators upload an existing assignment and get a scored report on how easily AI could complete it, plus concrete redesign suggestions. It reflects a shift from catching AI use after the fact toward redesigning assignments so cheating stops being the path of least resistance.
*For educators:* If a free tool can flag which of your assignments AI could finish in seconds, what does that reveal about what the assignment was actually testing?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/i-used-ai-to-build-ai-resistant-assignments

**United Kingdom — University of Chester (Global South cohort)** — Engineering management lecturers redesigned a master's-level module — taken largely by students from the Global South — around a real-world design problem specifically so generative AI's limitations become visible rather than hidden. Students must observe an actual process, gather original evidence, and propose a feasible redesign, drawing a clear line between what AI can assist with and what only their own judgement can produce.
*For educators:* For students still building fluency in academic English and unfamiliar assessment conventions, does exposing AI's limits teach responsible use — or just stack a second high-stakes skill on top of the first?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/heres-how-show-students-what-responsible-ai-use-looks

**United States — Delaware Pathways Middle Grades Grant** — After winning a $150,000 Delaware Pathways grant, a district's team designing a career-exploration and social-emotional-learning program deliberately stepped outside the K-12 "silo" to talk with technologists, scientists and entrepreneurs about how they actually use AI, rather than guessing from inside education alone. The resulting program treats AI fluency as secondary to durable human skills — collaboration, critical thinking, adaptability — built through relationships technology can't replace.
*For educators:* If people closest to AI adoption outside education say relationships matter more than tool literacy, why does most K-12 AI policy still start with the tool?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/the-k-12-silo-wont-survive-ai-but-our-relationships-might
