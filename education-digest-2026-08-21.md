# Education Digest — August 21, 2026

**United Kingdom** — The University of Southampton will not renew its contract with Turnitin beyond the end of the 2026-27 academic year, after the plagiarism-detection vendor proposed changes that would have let it use students' submitted work to train its AI services. The move signals a growing willingness among universities to walk away from long-standing edtech vendors over data-rights terms, not just over detection accuracy.
*For educators:* If a familiar tool changed its terms to let your students' work train someone else's AI, would you know — and would you have a say before it happened?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/southampton-dumps-turnitin-over-use-students-work-train-ai

**United States** — A new CoSN State of EdTech report finds 79% of K-12 districts now have AI guidelines in place, up sharply from 57% in 2025, yet teachers still report they lack the training and classroom-ready support to act on those policies. The gap suggests districts are moving faster on paperwork than on the coaching and modeling teachers actually need.
*For educators:* Does your school have an AI policy teachers could recite, or one they could actually use on a Tuesday morning with 28 kids in front of them?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/ai-is-in-schools-teachers-are-not-ready

**Hong Kong, Mainland China & the Philippines** — Universities across Asia are seeing surging enrollment in courses that pair philosophy with AI: the University of Hong Kong's master's in AI, ethics and society has drawn 400 students in three years, a course on the philosophy of AI at Peking University has become a campus staple, and De La Salle University in the Philippines says intake for its philosophy-and-AI bachelor's has quadrupled. Tech employers are reportedly hiring philosophy graduates specifically to work through questions of machine intelligence and consciousness.
*For educators:* As AI tools get easier to operate, is your curriculum investing enough in the harder skill — teaching students to reason about what these systems should and shouldn't be trusted to do?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/asia-embraces-philosophy-and-ai-courses
