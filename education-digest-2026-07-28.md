# AI in Education Digest — 2026-07-28

**Hong Kong / China / Philippines — university philosophy-and-AI degrees** — Times Higher Education reports that combined philosophy-and-AI courses are surging across Asia: the University of Hong Kong's MA in AI, ethics and society has drawn 400 students over three years, Fudan University in Shanghai just launched a double major in AI and philosophy, and De La Salle University in the Philippines says incoming first-year intake for its philosophy-and-AI bachelor's has quadrupled. Students in the piece say they need grounding in philosophical reasoning to make progress on technical AI problems, not just coding skill.
*For educators:* If understanding AI well enough to question it turns out to require philosophy as much as programming, should "AI literacy" courses at your institution look more like an ethics seminar than a coding bootcamp?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/asia-embraces-philosophy-and-ai-courses

**Argentina — Universidad Austral, Faculty of Business Sciences** — Times Higher Education Campus describes a case study in which faculty Belén Pagone and Cecilia Primogerio redesigned assessments by co-creating them with GenAI and students together, using the tool to brainstorm and prototype assessment formats rather than just grade finished work, on the premise that messy, iterative co-creation — not a quick AI-generated answer — is what builds students' judgement.
*For educators:* Would inviting students to help design the assessment itself, with AI as a shared brainstorming tool, change how much ownership they feel over the final task?
Source: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/how-genai-can-amplify-cocreation-higher-education

**United States — This Week with EdSurge podcast** — EdSurge features Rita Finkel of the Armory Foundation's college-prep program and Cobretti Williams of EdSurge's Voices of Change Fellowship making the case that skipping the "slow" work — struggling through a first draft, redoing a weak essay — is where the real value of a degree or a writing practice comes from, since that growth can't be replicated by having AI produce a polished result instantly.
*For educators:* Where in your own courses might a fast AI-generated shortcut be quietly erasing the exact struggle that used to produce the most growth?
Source: https://www.edsurge.com/news/what-does-ai-cost-when-we-skip-the-work
